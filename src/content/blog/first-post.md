When I was reading Tao Te Ching by Lao Tzu, a single line struck me: “The master doesn’t try to be powerful, yet he is truly powerful.” When I first read it, I was confused. How can someone be powerful without trying to be powerful?

It’s natural to think this way, especially today, because we live in a digital world full of ideas like “aura farming,” where people constantly try to appear strong or influential. In such a world, an ancient text telling you not to show off your power seems counterintuitive.

But when I reflected on it, it started to make sense. It reminded me of something Buddha once said: “Three things cannot be long hidden: the sun, the moon, and the truth.” When you consider this alongside Lao Tzu’s quote, a pattern emerges.

Let’s examine this more closely. If something is real and true, it is naturally apparent. No one needs to force belief in it. Do we “believe” in the sun? No, because it’s obvious to everyone—we see it every day. Belief is only required for things that are hidden or uncertain, like the concept of God. In essence, belief is a way of affirming what is not obvious.

So, if someone’s power is real and evident, why would they need to force it? Displaying it is just a different form of trying to convince others of something that is already clear. In the digital age, if you truly know your worth, there is no need to prove it—it will show naturally.

True power, like the sun or the truth, doesn’t need to be flaunted. It is obvious to those who can see it, and forcing it only diminishes its authenticity.
